# Ampacity chart

Allowable amps for copper and aluminum building wire by insulation rating, at 30 °C (86 °F) with up to three current-carrying conductors. To apply derating, use the [wire size calculator](/wire-size-calculator). {.lead}

{{chart}}

## Reading the chart

summary: The column you can use depends on the terminals as well as the wire. {.open}

- Use the column of the lowest-rated part of the circuit. Most breakers and panel terminals are rated 60 °C or 75 °C (110.14(C)), so 90 °C wire is usually credited at 75 °C.
- The 90 °C column is the starting point for derating 90 °C wire, before the terminal limit is applied.
- 14, 12 and 10 AWG copper can't be protected above 15, 20 and 30 A, whatever their column says (240.4(D)).

## Code editions

summary: {{editions}} {.open}

The ampacity values didn't change. In NEC 2017 the table is numbered 310.15(B)(16) and the correction and adjustment tables 310.15(B)(2)(a) and 310.15(B)(3)(a). NEC 2026 adds a row for 16 AWG copper.
